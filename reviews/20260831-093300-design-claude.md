## 1. Aesthetic quality — 4/5

Genuinely competitive, and against part of the set it wins. `docusign/01_pricing_overview.png` is a purple gradient hero, pill buttons, four rounded cards and a mint "You're saving up to 33%" flag — dated generic SaaS. The Pumasi products page is more confident than that. Against `ngrok_pages/01_homepage_hero.png` (gradient-glow hero, serif/sans mix) it's a peer, not a superior — different position, executed cleanly.

Costs it the fifth point: the `--art-*` mosaic at the head of every product card in `pumasi-products-page.png`. Thirty-odd saturated blocks of `--art-1` green and `--art-2` amber, no information, and near-identical across all three cards. It's the one place the system does the decoration it says it doesn't do.

## 2. Visual hierarchy — 3/5

On the specimen sheet it's excellent — the badge ladder reads as a ladder at a glance, and the four button ranks are unmistakably four ranks while sharing the `>` caret.

On the one real surface it inverts. In `pumasi-products-page.png` the loudest object on each card is that mosaic; the product name (`--text-lg`) and the `[BETA]`/`[SEED]`/`[ALPHA]` badge — the two things the page exists to convey — sit below it and quieter. Calendly's booking page and DocuSign's pricing page both put their most important object first; this one puts decoration first.

## 3. Contrast and accessibility — 3/5

The commons palette checks out. All eight semantic values and all six signals recompute to the two decimals now in §4 — `--accent` 5.26/10.21, `--warn` 5.29/9.26, `--danger` 6.40/7.00, signals 5.75–6.70 light and 8.99–10.58 dark. `--ink-faint` at 3.26 is correctly flagged and correctly licensed.

Two of the doc's own stated floors are broken in `product-theme.css`, the new file:

**The light-mode terminal.** `.terminal` sets `background: var(--ink-strong)` (#0a0a09) in light, but the semantic spans keep their light-ground values:

| `product-theme.css` | on #0a0a09 |
|---|---|
| `.terminal .ok` (L222) | **3.67:1** |
| `.terminal .warn` (L223) | **3.65:1** |

All under 4.5:1; Sign's `$` is under 3:1. Dark is fine — L225 restates the ground. It is only light that's broken, and §8 specifically promises Tunnel's light scheme "is a real second scheme and is checked, not assumed."

**Control boundaries.** §4 says "non-text boundaries ≥ 3:1" in the same paragraph that credits `--ink-faint` for clearing it. `--line-strong` #b9b9b0 (`theme.css:31`) is 1.92:1 on `--paper` and 1.98:1 on `--paper-raised` — and it is the entire visible boundary of `.input`/`.select`/`.textarea` (`product-theme.css:107`) and of `.slot`, the control Booking is judged on.

## 4. Consistency — 4/5

This part holds, and it's the strongest thing here. One `--radius: 0`, one badge ladder, one four-rank button set, and elevation genuinely re-expressed rather than replaced — `--elev-2` resolving to a border on the document surface and a shadow in the app with no markup change is the right mechanism. `--signal` is bound from `data-product` and never named downstream.

Two deductions. `--signal` has no shipped consumer: `card-signal` and `data-product` appear at exactly one place in the codebase, `src/render/styleguide.ts:190`, the styleguide's own demo. The products page renders three product cards with no signal edge at all. And `--ok` is literally `--accent` (`theme.css:46`), so "verified" and "press this" are the same green — which softens principle 1's claim that a reader learns green means the thing to press.

## 5. Asset craft — 4/5

The theming mechanism is correct and I verified it across all nine files: well-formed XML, zero `--` inside comments, every paint routed through a `--pm-*` indirection declared as `var(--token, #literal)` with a `prefers-color-scheme` block restating the literals (26 fallbacks per diagram, 6–8 per mark). That genuinely resolves inline *and* in `<img>` in both schemes — a real solution to a problem most systems get wrong.

At 16px (zoomed from `pumasi-brand-light.png`): booking is the best — six cells, one clay cell, reads instantly. Commons and sign hold their silhouettes. Tunnel is the weak one: the three mux diagonals at 2-unit stroke thin to near-hairlines and it reads as a faint arrow rather than three streams converging.

## The strongest argument against merging

**The half of the system that fixes the actual problem has no rendered evidence at all.** The fragmentation described in §1 lived in the three product apps, and the fix for it lives in `product-theme.css`. But all three PNGs in the gate package are commons surfaces — the specimen sheet and the products page. `.terminal`, `.slot`, `.input`, `.seal`, `.menu`, `.modal` are not rendered in the styleguide, not screenshotted, and not covered by `test/theme.test.ts` (which asserts token presence and that `base.css` holds no colour — a good contract, but it computes no ratios). That is precisely why the light-terminal failures shipped: nobody looked at a light terminal. This is being merged as "one system with four surfaces" on evidence from one surface.

Both defects are narrow and fixable — restate `.ok`/`.warn`/`--signal` inside the light `.terminal` block against its inverted ground, and either raise `--line-strong` to 3:1 or stop claiming the 3:1 floor for boundaries. But they are in a document whose credibility rests on having measured, and which was corrected mid-review to prove it had.

VERDICT: OBJECT — `assets/brand/product-theme.css:221-223`: in light mode `.terminal` inverts to a `--ink-strong` #0a0a09 ground while `.ok` (#197a3d, 3.67:1), `.warn` (#9a5b00, 3.65:1) and the `$` prompt in `--signal` (sign #2350c8, **2.88:1**) keep their light-ground values, all below the 4.5:1 floor DESIGN_SYSTEM.md §4 calls "not negotiable"; compounded by `--line-strong` #b9b9b0 (`assets/theme.css:31`) at 1.92:1 serving as the sole boundary of `.input` (`product-theme.css:107`) and `.slot` against the same section's stated 3:1 non-text minimum.

## One thing in the fix itself

`test/contrast.test.ts` does not catch the defect it was written for. I reintroduced the original `.terminal` verbatim in a scratch copy — `background: var(--ink-strong); color: var(--paper);`, no restated tokens — and the suite stayed green, 6/6:

```
ok 5 - the terminal's own ramp clears AA against the terminal's own ground
```

Two reasons, both in `schemes()`. `terminal` is seeded from the light `:root` map, so `terminal.get(token)` is truthy for every token whether or not `.terminal` restates it — the `assert.ok(fg, ".terminal does not restate …")` guard can never fire. And `ground` is read as the token *named* `--paper-sunken` rather than from the `background:` declaration, so with the ground moved by a property the test measures light ink on light paper and reports 5.0:1.

It does catch the narrower case: deleting `--ok` from the restated ramp fails test 5. So the guard covers "a foreground dropped from the ramp" and not "the ground moved out from under the ramp," which is the shape of what shipped. The file's docstring — "every pair the system actually puts on screen … recomputed from the stylesheets," "a colour cannot be changed without this file agreeing" — overstates that, and finding #3's claim that it was verified to fail on reintroduction does not hold.

The remedy is small: parse `background:\s*var\((--[a-z-]+)\)` out of the `.terminal` block and resolve the ground through it, and build the `terminal` map without seeding it from `light` so the "does not restate" assertion can bite. The shipped CSS is correct today; the guard on it is not the one described.

VERDICT: OBJECT — `test/contrast.test.ts` `schemes()` reads the terminal's ground from the token named `--paper-sunken` rather than from its `background:` declaration, and seeds the terminal map from `light`, so the original defect (`background: var(--ink-strong)` with an unrestated ramp) passes the suite 6/6 when reintroduced.

## Two probes of my own, since the last summary was true only of the shape it tested

**Does the ground really follow the declaration, or is `--ink-strong` just special-cased by luck?** Kept the full restated ramp and changed only `background: var(--paper-sunken)` → `var(--ink-strong)`. It reported the ground as `--ink-strong (#f2f7f4)` — the terminal's *own* restated value, not the page's, and not a hardcoded `--paper-sunken`. Both pages fail at 1.27:1. `terminalGround` and `resolve()` are both doing real work; neither is decorative.

**Can it go silently green?** A literal `background: #070908` (no `var()`) trips `assert.ok(background, ...)` and takes down all six surface tests with an explicit message rather than skipping. Deleting the block entirely trips `blockAfter`'s `could not find .terminal {`. No vacuous-pass path I could find.

`resolve()`'s fallback arm is only exercised when a token is genuinely missing — which is the point, and is what (b) covers. The docstring's own limit statement ("a pair that nobody adds here is a pair nobody checks") is accurate and now matches §4's prose, which no longer claims the list is automatic. §4's account of the third failure — a guard written from the fix rather than from the failure — is recorded plainly and in the open, as you said.
